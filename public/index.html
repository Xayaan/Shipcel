How it works

Shipcel is the control panel. Everything it sets up runs in your own Cloudflare account.

  1. You

    Push to GitHub, run shipcel, drop a folder or ask your AI agent.

  2. Shipcel

    Builds your app, sets up what it needs and checks every deploy.

  3. Your Cloudflare account

    • Your app
    • Database
    • File storage
    • Key-value data
    • Sign-in
    • Realtime

  4. Your visitors

    Served from Cloudflare’s network around the world.

Stop using Shipcel and your apps keep running: they’re in your account.

No infrastructure code

  • Set up for you: Workers, builds, databases, storage, domains, secrets, sign-in and realtime, created and connected in your account. You never configure Cloudflare by hand.
  • Reviewed first: every change is shown before it happens, and recorded afterwards.
  • Daily work in the dashboard: secrets, domains, rollbacks, migrations, backups and previews.
  • Ready to deploy: starter kits deploy as they are, and the CLI recognises your framework.

Every feature, and what isn't ready yet

No surprise bills

Shipcel watches what every app costs, so a bug that loops can’t become a bill.

  • Free-only mode, on by default. Nothing a project can be billed for, such as file storage or containers, is created.
  • A budget for every app. $0 beyond what Cloudflare includes, unless you change it. Alerts in the dashboard, Slack, Discord or any webhook.
  • Alert only, or pause. An app that goes over can be paused: visitors see that, its schedules stop and its data stays, until you resume it.
  • The free plan stops instead of billing. Workers, D1 and KV stop at their daily limits until midnight UTC.

In your Cloudflare account

Shipcel adds no limits of its own below these. Your apps get Cloudflare's full free allowance:

  • Server code (Workers): 100,000 requests a day, 10 ms CPU time per request
  • Static files: Free and unlimited requests
  • D1 SQL databases: 5 GB in total, up to 10 databases of 500 MB; 5 million rows read and 100,000 rows written a day; 7 days of point-in-time restore
  • Postgres and MySQL through Hyperdrive: 100,000 queries a day to a database hosted elsewhere, which bills you itself
  • KV key-value data: 100,000 reads and 1,000 writes a day; 1 GB stored
  • R2 file storage: 10 GB stored, 1 million writes and 10 million reads a month; no egress fees. R2 bills usage beyond this even on the free plan
  • Durable Objects (realtime): 100,000 requests and 13,000 GB-seconds a day; idle WebSocket connections hibernate at no cost
  • Builds: 3,000 build minutes a month, one build at a time
  • Email sending: Not offered
  • Containers: Not available

Workers, D1 and KV stop at their daily limits until midnight UTC instead of billing you. R2 is pay-as-you-go beyond its free tier.

Shipcel stays free, as a launch offer. Every price here is Cloudflare's, charged by Cloudflare at its list prices: no markup, no platform fee, no per-seat pricing.
